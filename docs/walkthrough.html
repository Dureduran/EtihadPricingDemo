The answer

02 / 16
Do not leave the New Model live. Return to Current Pricing until the test is healthy — and treat the decision system as the product, not the algorithm.

Situation

Etihad already has an in-house ancillary dynamic-pricing capability. Extra baggage and extra-legroom are the v1 products. Fare-brand upgrades are a later expansion, not this lab.

Complication

A model that prints a price is not the job. The job is to test a New Model against Current Pricing, keep Business Rules in final control, introduce it in traffic steps, and HOLD or Return when it is not healthy.

Resolution

A production lab: monotonic XGBoost estimates P(buy), a price grid maximises expected revenue, Business Rules finish, a four-layer fallback protects checkout, and the monitor currently says Return to Current Pricing.

What I assumed (explicit)

  • Current Pricing is a rules surface on route, days to departure, product, channel, remaining extra-legroom — a stand-in, not Etihad logic.
  • Guests buy if latent WTP ≥ displayed price. That WTP is synthetic, with haul, scarcity, party size and channel baked in.
  • Displayed prices in the log are Current Pricing plus AED −20 to +25 jitter, so the New Model sees a range, not a single tariff.
  • Fare-brand inclusion and Gold/Platinum extra-legroom comps are portfolio stand-ins from public merchandising patterns.

What that implies for the numbers

  • Every metric in this deck is a simulated result using synthetic/public data.
  • The New Model can overfit the generator’s WTP shape even though it never sees the hidden columns.
  • Training prices are Current Pricing quotes, not the New Model’s own quotes — so scoring a new grid is an extrapolation.
  • Until a real offer log exists, AUC is a lab diagnostic, not a go-live certificate.

Pricing mechanics

09 / 16
The New Model does not emit a price. It scores every allowed price, picks the expected-revenue maximum, then Business Rules decide what the guest actually sees.
price* = argmaxp ∈ grid   p × P(buy | p, booking)    then    customer_price = BusinessRules(price*)
1. Booking context
2. Score P(buy) on grid
3. Max price × P(buy)
4. Business Rules
5. Guest sees AED

Grids (AED)

Extra baggage 40–220 step 10.
Extra-legroom 50–280 step 10.
RM bands can shrink the grid (AUH–LHR extra-legroom live band 80–150).

Why a grid, not a continuous price

Ancillaries are merchandised in round AED. A grid is auditable, band-able, and cheap to score. Continuous optimisation would imply a precision the channel does not have.

Worked example (cap)

New Model recommends AED 175. Temporary RM cap is AED 150. Guest sees AED 150. Reason code: temporary_rm_cap. The model did not “lose.” Rules won, by design.

What AUC is allowed to mean

The New Model ranks buyers vs non-buyers on held-out offer-log rows. It does not prove that the prices it would have shown — which were not in the log — would have raised revenue. Discrimination ≠ commercial impact.

Business Rules (always last)

  • No inventory → do not offer
  • Included in fare → AED 0
  • Gold/Platinum Economy extra-legroom → complimentary stand-in
  • Min / max and temporary RM caps
  • Airport bags never cheaper than digital after rules

Checkout fallback

If the New Model throws, checkout does not fail open.

  1. New Model
  2. Current Pricing
  3. Simple pricing rules
  4. Safe fixed price (75 / 95)

Monitor gates

  • Return if RevPP ≤ −2% or conversion ≤ −3 pp
  • HOLD if AUH–BOM, DTD ≤ 7, prediction−realised > 8 pp
  • Expand only if RevPP > +4% and conversion holds
  • Fail if fallback rate ≥ 3% or pricing failures ≥ 2%
  • Fail if included/comp offers are billed

Week one on the job — and what is deliberately not v1

15 / 16
I would not retrain first. I would request the real offer log, wire it into this same decision system, and only then discuss a more ambitious model.

Week one — data

  1. Displayed ancillary price, product, channel, timestamp
  2. Purchase / refund / void outcomes
  3. Fare brand at ticketing and Guest tier at offer time
  4. Seat-map remaining extra-legroom / preferred inventory
  5. Current Pricing output and any existing model score
  6. Digital vs airport fulfilment prices
  7. Guardrail / override history from Revenue Management

Week one — method

  • Fit a GLM baseline on the real log before any boosting.
  • Keep the monotone price constraint; keep Business Rules last.
  • Shadow only until calibration and rule-violation rates are boring.
  • Define HOLD markets in advance (the lab uses AUH–BOM, DTD ≤ 7 as the worked example).

Spoken 90-day ideas — not built

  • Fare-brand buy-up (Basic → Value / Comfort) as a third product.
  • Fare + ancillary coherence: ticket bid price vs bag/seat so the bundle does not fight itself.
  • A Deepair-style end-to-end net as a later live test, not a replacement for this control plane.

Risks I would name: Guest perception, airport vs digital bag price, fare/ancillary inconsistency, overfit to synthetic WTP until the real log arrives.
