02What we are actually deciding

Revenue Management already has Current Pricing. The production question is not what price this passenger should see. It is whether the New Model deserves more than 20 percent of traffic on AUH to LHR extra legroom, or whether we should Return to Current Pricing.

The choice

Expand, HOLD, or Return. One booking with a better expected revenue is not a production decision. The batch is.

The gates

Return if revenue per passenger falls 2 percent or more, or if conversion falls 3 percentage points or more. Expand only if revenue per passenger rises more than 4 percent and conversion holds.

03How I approached it

I started from the commercial decision and worked backward to the algorithm, so the New Model can be switched off without breaking checkout.

Choice Why If I had done the opposite
Treat Current Pricing as the baseline Etihad already prices ancillaries in house. The lab has to inherit that quote and improve it. A pretty dashboard that cannot be compared with today’s price.
Model purchase chance, not the price itself Revenue Management still needs a grid, a band, and a cap. Probability is the input. Price is the decision. An opaque number with no lever when a route needs a ceiling.
Business Rules always finish Brand inclusion, Guest comps, inventory and RM caps are not suggestions. The model sells a seat that is already included, or a seat that does not exist.
A traffic ladder, not a cutover Shadow logs the New Model while the guest still sees Current Pricing. Then 5 percent, 20 percent, 50 percent, 100 percent. One bad week becomes a network wide ancillary event.
The monitor decides HOLD, expand, or Return A locally clever price on AUH to LHR is not permission to keep the model live. A price that looks smart on one booking and loses money in the batch.
Say the data is simulated, every time This is a portfolio prototype. Honesty is part of the design. A number that sounds like Etihad production and is not.

What I assumed

  • Current Pricing is a rules surface on route, days to departure, product, channel, and remaining extra legroom. It is a placeholder, not Etihad logic.
  • Guests buy when their willingness to pay is at least the displayed price. That willingness to pay is synthetic, with haul, scarcity, party size and channel baked in.
  • Displayed prices in the log are Current Pricing plus a little jitter, so the New Model sees a range rather than a single tariff.
  • Fare brand inclusion and Gold or Platinum extra legroom comps follow public merchandising patterns. They are not Etihad production rules.

What that means for the numbers

  • Every metric on this page is a simulated result using synthetic and public data.
  • The New Model can still overfit the shape of that synthetic willingness to pay even though it never sees the hidden columns.
  • Training prices are Current Pricing quotes, not the New Model’s own quotes, so scoring a new grid is an extrapolation.
  • Until a real offer log exists, AUC is a lab diagnostic, not a licence to go live.

05Why this model

I chose monotonic XGBoost because Revenue Management can trust it, test it, export it, and switch it off. Not because it is the most fashionable architecture.

06How a price is made

The New Model does not emit a price. It scores every allowed price, picks the expected revenue maximum, then Business Rules decide what the guest actually sees.

price* = argmaxp in grid   p × P(buy | p, booking)    then    customer price = Business Rules(price*)
1. Booking context
2. Score purchase chance
3. Max expected revenue
4. Business Rules
5. Guest sees AED

Grids in AED

Extra baggage from 40 to 220, step 10. Extra legroom from 50 to 280, step 10. RM bands can shrink that grid. The live AUH to LHR extra legroom band is 80 to 150.

Why a grid

Ancillaries are merchandised in round AED. A grid is auditable, easy to band, and cheap to score. A continuous optimum would imply a precision the channel does not have.

Worked example

The New Model recommends AED 175. The temporary RM cap is AED 150. The guest sees AED 150. The model did not lose. Rules won, by design.

Offline, I train purchase chance on Databricks Free, and on a parallel BigQuery path in GCP, from the same offer log. Online, the lab scores an exported model. I am not claiming a paid serving endpoint.

Train

Seed the log, keep only columns a production offer log could have, fit monotonic XGBoost, export the model. Databricks and BigQuery hold the same two tables: the raw log and the training log.

Decide

Booking, then the New Model grid, then Business Rules, then log and monitor. If anything throws, checkout walks New Model, then Current Pricing, then simple rules, then a safe fixed price of AED 75 for bags and AED 95 for seats.

Why a good fixture can fail a batch

  • The comparison is the New Model after Business Rules versus Current Pricing quotes. Included and complimentary offers record AED 0 on the New Model side.

09Controls that keep it safe

Business Rules, a four layer fallback, and a traffic ladder are the product. The New Model is a component that can be set to zero without stranding a guest. Revenue Management sets traffic, bands, and pause. This is not per passenger approval.

Business Rules, always last

  • No inventory: do not offer
  • Included in the fare: AED 0
  • Gold or Platinum Economy extra legroom: complimentary, following public merchandising patterns
  • Min, max, and temporary RM caps
  • Airport bags never cheaper than digital after rules

Checkout fallback

If the New Model throws, checkout does not fail open.

  1. New Model
  2. Current Pricing
  3. Simple pricing rules
  4. Safe fixed price, AED 75 bags or AED 95 seats

Monitor gates

  • Return if RevPP falls 2 percent or more, or conversion falls 3 percentage points or more
  • HOLD if AUH to BOM within 7 days of departure drifts by more than 8 percentage points
  • Expand only if RevPP rises more than 4 percent and conversion holds
  • Fail if fallback rate is 3 percent or more, or pricing failures are 2 percent or more
  • Fail if included or complimentary offers are billed

11What I would do in week one

I would not retrain first. I would request the real offer log, wire it into this same decision system, and only then discuss a more ambitious model.

Data I would ask for

  1. Displayed ancillary price, product, channel, timestamp
  2. Purchase, refund, and void outcomes
  3. Fare brand at ticketing and Guest tier at offer time
  4. Remaining extra legroom on the seat map
  5. Current Pricing output and any existing model score
  6. Digital versus airport fulfilment prices
  7. Guardrail and override history from Revenue Management

Method I would keep

  • Fit a simple baseline on the real log before any boosting.
  • Keep the monotone price constraint. Keep Business Rules last.
  • Stay in shadow until calibration and rule violation rates are boring.
  • Name HOLD markets in advance. The lab uses AUH to BOM within 7 days of departure as the worked example.

Later, not built here

  • Fare brand buy up from Basic to Value or Comfort as a third product.
  • Fare and ancillary coherence, so the ticket bid price and the bag or seat price do not fight each other.
  • An end to end neural net as a later live test, not a replacement for this control plane.

Risks I would name on day one: Guest perception, airport versus digital bag price, fare and ancillary inconsistency, and overfitting to synthetic willingness to pay until the real log arrives.

12What I would tell Revenue Management

Recommendation

On this simulated log the New Model is not commercially healthy. Return to Current Pricing. The AUH to LHR fixture looked encouraging. The batch did not. That disagreement is why the lab is a monitor, not a score dashboard. Keep the decision system. Retrain and retest on a real offer log before any expansion past shadow.

Keep

Purchase chance on a grid. Monotone price. Business Rules last. Shadow ladder. Four layer fallback. HOLD and Return gates. Leakage tests. AED. The words Current Pricing and New Model.

Do not keep live

This particular trained model, on this simulated log, at 20 percent traffic. Commercial impact is −44.3% RevPP and −19.8 percentage points of conversion, against a contract of −2% and −3 percentage points.
